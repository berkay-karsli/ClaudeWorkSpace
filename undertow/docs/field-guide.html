Undertow

An asymmetric strategy game for Android, built on Root's bones and set on a coral reef. Ten factions fight over twelve reefs, and no two of them play by the same rules.

2–4 factions per game 60–90 minutes Solo vs AI bots Pass-and-play on one phone 30 VP to win

Shared rules

How the reef works

Everything Root players know carries over. The reef adds currents, gates and the Trench, which several factions are built around.

Twelve reefs, three suits

Every reef is Kelp, Coral or Pearl and has one to three nooks for buildings. Channels connect them.

A turn has three tides

Flood to gather and prepare, Slack to act, Ebb to score and draw. They replace Birdsong, Daylight and Evening.

Rule and movement

You rule a reef when you have the most warriors and buildings in it. To move, you must rule where you leave or where you arrive.

Currentsnew

Moving along a current arrow never needs rule. Four currents circle the middle of the map as the Gyre, and the Immortal Bloom can add more.

Battle

Roll two 12-sided clash dice showing 0 to 3. The attacker keeps the higher, the defender the lower, each capped by their warriors present. A defender with no warriors takes one extra hit. Undertow cards ambush before the roll.

Gates and the Trenchnew

The four corner reefs open to the ocean, which matters to the Silver Shoal. Three southern reefs sit on the rim of the Trench, home of the Lantern Abyss.

The Tide deck

54 cards in Kelp, Coral, Pearl and wild Moon. Craft items for points (Fins, Net, Spear, Harpoon, Chisel, Lantern, Pearl, Kelp Tonic) or adaptations that bend a rule for you.

Winning

First to 30 VP. Removing an enemy building or token scores 1 VP. Or play a Tidal Dominance card and win by ruling three reefs of its suit at the start of your turn. Moon Dominance needs two opposite gates.

Specimen plates

The factions in detail

Complexity runs from one to four dots. Reach is how much of the map a faction naturally holds, which the game uses to suggest fair lineups.

Plate I · Predator

The Blood Tide

Carcharhinus amblyrhynchos · grey reef sharks

Complexity
Reach
8

“Stop swimming and you drown.”

Ten sharks and nothing else. They have no homes worth defending and no hands to craft with. They go where the blood is, and they score by feeding.

How it plays

  • Keep swimming. At your Ebb, any shark that did not move this turn drowns.
  • Apex. Each shark counts as two warriors for rule and can deal up to two hits. You only have ten of them.
  • Blood in the water. Whenever a battle anywhere removes a piece, a Blood token drops in that reef. Your sharks can reach a Blood reef from two channels away, ignoring rule.
  • Frenzy. At Flood, your Frenzy equals the Blood on the map, up to 5. Each point is an extra action. At Frenzy 4 or more you must battle in every reef where your sharks share water with Blood.
  • No hands. You can't craft. Cards pay for actions and power your battles.

How it scores

  • At Ebb, eat each Blood token in a reef with your sharks: 1 VP each.
  • 1 VP for every 3 Silver Shoal fish you remove.
  • Nurseries score when built: 1, 2, 3.

A turn

Flood
Set Frenzy. Each Nursery adds a pup, which has to swim out this turn.
Slack
3 actions plus Frenzy: move, battle, build a Nursery.
Ebb
Feed, drown the sharks that stayed put, draw a card.

Field noteMany sharks breathe by swimming, pushing water over their gills. For them, stopping means suffocating.

HuntsSilver Shoal, Siphon Chain. FearsImmortal Bloom, Lantern Abyss.

Plate II · Racer

The Silver Shoal

Sardinops sagax · sardines on their run

Complexity
Reach
4

“Ten thousand fish, one direction.”

A school with no interest in holding the reef. Fish pour in through one gate, cross the map, and score by leaving through another.

How it plays

  • The run. At Flood, fish arrive at any gate: 3, plus 1 for each card you discard of that gate's suit.
  • One mind. All your fish in a reef are one school and move together, leaving no stragglers. Once per turn a school may split in two.
  • Fast water. A school moves up to two channels per move and ignores rule on the second step.
  • Bait ball. When attacked, a school may scatter into an adjacent reef instead of fighting. The attacker takes one fish for every four in the school.
  • Small mouths. A school deals at most one hit per three fish.

How it scores

  • At Ebb, a school standing on a different gate from the one it entered by may leave the map: 1–3 fish score 1 VP, 4–6 score 2, 7–9 score 3, 10 or more score 5.
  • Fish that leave return to your supply for the next run.

A turn

Flood
The run arrives.
Slack
3 moves. A school in a Kelp reef can feed instead and draw a card.
Ebb
Schools exit and score, then draw a card.

Field noteEvery winter billions of sardines run up the east coast of South Africa, and every predator on that coast follows them.

Lovesthe Bloom's currents. FearsBlood Tide, Lantern Abyss.

Plate III · Builder

The Coral Commonwealth

Acropora cervicornis · staghorn coral and kin

Complexity
Reach
9

“We never move. We grow.”

A living reef that never takes a step. It grows outward one coral head at a time and takes the map by becoming part of it.

How it plays

  • Rooted. Polyps can't move. They spread only by spawning.
  • Three corals, three engines. Fan coral filters food into Calcium. Staghorn coral houses new polyps. Brain coral draws cards. You build with Calcium, and only in reefs next to coral you already have.
  • Mass spawning. Once per turn, discard a card: every reef of that suit holding your coral sends up to two polyps into neighboring reefs. A Moon card spawns everywhere, and the spawn rides currents two steps.
  • Stinging cells. When defending in a reef with your coral, you deal one extra hit.
  • Bleaching. Coral with no polyps in its reef at Ebb bleaches. It makes and scores nothing until a polyp returns.

How it scores

  • Each coral head scores when placed, on its own rising track: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5.
  • At Ebb, your largest connected patch of coral reefs: 3 reefs score 1 VP, 5 score 2, 7 or more score 3.

A turn

Flood
1 Calcium per Fan coral.
Slack
3 actions: grow, spawn, battle or draw.
Ebb
Each Staghorn adds a polyp. Draw 1 card plus 1 per two Brain corals. Check for bleaching.

Field noteMany corals spawn together on one or two nights a year, timed by the moon. That is why a Moon card sets off a mass spawn.

FearsSandwright Clans, Chromatic Court.

Plate IV · Drifter

The Immortal Bloom

Turritopsis dohrnii · the immortal jellyfish

Complexity
Reach
5

“We drift. We multiply. We come back.”

Jellyfish can't steer. They drift, they multiply, and they don't stay dead. You never command them directly. You bend the currents that carry them.

How it plays

  • Drift. At your Ebb, every jellyfish moves one channel along the current leaving its reef, printed or yours. Drifting ignores rule. With no current, they stay.
  • Your currents. Place or turn up to two of your four current arrows each turn. They work for everyone: any faction may move along them without ruling.
  • Sting. When enemy warriors move into a reef holding two or more jellyfish, their owner removes one of the arrivals.
  • Immortal. Removed jellyfish go to your reversion pool, not the supply. At Flood they come back beside your polyp stalks.
  • Bloom. Discard a card: each reef of that suit with a polyp stalk gains jellyfish equal to the stalks there. A Moon card blooms everywhere.

How it scores

  • 1 VP per enemy warrior your stings remove, up to 3 a round.
  • At Ebb, after drifting, 1 VP per reef with four or more jellyfish (a smack).
  • Polyp stalks score when built: 1, 2, 2, 3, 4.

A turn

Flood
Reverted jellyfish return.
Slack
Set currents, bloom, build a stalk (a reef with 3+ jellyfish, discard a card of its suit), or battle once.
Ebb
Drift, score smacks, draw a card.

Field noteTurritopsis dohrnii can turn back into a polyp when injured or starving and start its life over.

HuntsBlood Tide, Silver Shoal. FearsSandwright Clans, Octarch.

Plate V · Terraformer

The Sandwright Clans

Bolbometopon muricatum · bumphead parrotfish

Complexity
Reach
7

“Every beach was a reef once.”

Parrotfish chew coral and turn it into sand. These clans reshape the map itself: they close channels with sandbars and raise whole reefs out of the sea as islands.

How it plays

  • Graze. In a reef you rule, discard a card of its suit to eat an enemy building or token. Gain 2 Sand.
  • Grind. At Flood, gain 1 Sand per reef you rule.
  • Sandbar. Spend 2 Sand to close a channel beside a reef you rule. Only your fish can cross it, and jellyfish can't drift through it. Others dig it out by spending a move with warriors at either end.
  • Cay. Spend 5 Sand in a reef touched by two of your sandbars and it rises into an island. No one else can build or battle there for the rest of the game.
  • Mucus cocoon. At Ebb, pick one reef. Your fish there can't be attacked until your next turn, but they don't count for rule.

How it scores

  • 1 VP per enemy building or token eaten.
  • Cays score 2, 3, 4, 5.
  • At Ebb, 1 VP per three of your sandbars on the map.

A turn

Flood
Grind sand.
Slack
3 actions: move, battle, recruit, graze, sandbar or cay.
Ebb
Cocoon, draw 1 card plus 1 per cay.

Field noteMuch of the white sand on tropical beaches has passed through a parrotfish. Many species sleep inside a bubble of their own mucus.

HuntsCoral Commonwealth. FearsOctarch.

Plate VI · Merchant

The Hermit Syndicate

Dardanus · marine hermit crabs

Complexity
Reach
6

“Every shell has a price.”

Crabs living in borrowed houses who run the reef's only shell market. Everyone needs armor, everyone pays the crabs, and the payments fund the crabs' own turns.

How it plays

  • The market. Other factions shop at the start of their Slack and pay you in cards. A Shell absorbs the next hit on their warriors in one reef. Passage lets them move through your reefs this turn ignoring rule. Salvage sells them a crafted item from your stock.
  • Your prices. At Ebb, set each service's price from 1 to 4 cards.
  • Tithe. Cards paid to you become your actions. Each Tithe card buys one move, battle, recruit or scavenge. Building a Shell Market takes a Tithe card of the reef's suit.
  • Vacancy chain. Your shell line runs from small to large. Add a large shell and every shell moves up a size, and the smallest drops into your market stock. Large shells come from scavenging Pearl reefs and from customers' cracked shells.
  • Armored. While your line holds a large shell, your crabs ignore the first hit in each battle.

How it scores

  • 1 VP each time another faction buys from you, once per faction per turn.
  • Shell Markets score when built: 1, 2, 3, 4.
  • At Flood, 1 VP per three Tithe cards you're holding.

A turn

Flood
Score your savings. Each market moves the shell line once.
Slack
Spend Tithe on actions.
Ebb
Set prices, draw a card.

Field noteWhen a big empty shell turns up, hermit crabs queue by size and swap houses all the way down the line. Biologists call it a vacancy chain.

Sells toeveryone, especially whoever is losing. FearsOctarch.

Plate VII · Trapper

The Lantern Abyss

Ceratias holboelli · deep-sea anglerfish

Complexity
Reach
3

“Follow the light.”

A cult of anglerfish from the trench below the reef. They never live on the map. They hang lights over it, offer real rewards, and wait for someone to take the bait.

How it plays

  • The Trench. Your anglers live in the Trench, off the map. No one else can go there.
  • Lures. At Flood, place Lures (up to your Glow) within two channels of the Trench. Each shows an offer any faction can take. Treasure: end a move here and draw 2 cards. Shelter: warriors here can be attacked only by the Abyss. Glory: rule this reef at the end of your turn and score 1 VP.
  • Snap. At your Ebb, for each Lure with enemy warriors under it, anglers rise and strike before any dice, 1 hit per angler you commit. Then the battle is rolled as normal and your anglers sink back.
  • Gullet. Warriors you remove go into your Gullet instead of back to their owner. Each one digested gives you 1 Glow, then returns to its owner.
  • Fusion. Spend Glow to fuse a mate to your body for a permanent upgrade: a second light (+1 Lure), a wider jaw (+1 hit on Snap), or pressure sense (see every hand).
  • Snuffed. Any faction ruling a Lure's reef can snuff it out for 1 VP.

How it scores

  • At Flood, 1 VP per two warriors digested.
  • Fusions score as gained: 1, 1, 2, 2, 3, 3.

A turn

Flood
Digest, then place Lures.
Slack
Fuse, spawn anglers in the Trench by discarding cards, slide one Lure one channel.
Ebb
Snap, then draw a card.

Field noteIn some anglerfish the tiny male bites the female and fuses with her body for life, sharing her blood.

HuntsSilver Shoal, Blood Tide. Fearsanyone who grabs the bait and leaves before your Ebb.

Plate VIII · Serpent

The Siphon Chain

Praya dubia · giant siphonophore

Complexity
Reach
5

“Forty meters long, and of one mind.”

A siphonophore is a colony of tiny animals joined into one body. Your pieces form a single chain across the map that moves like a snake and shares one set of nerves.

How it plays

  • One body. Your zooids form an unbroken line through adjacent reefs, led by the Float.
  • Slither. Move the Float one channel, and every zooid shifts one place forward along the chain. Slithering ignores rule. You get one slither per Swimmer zooid.
  • Shared nerves. In a battle anywhere along the chain, you can deal hits up to the number of Stingers in the whole chain, not only the ones in that reef.
  • Specialists. Swimmers move you. Stingers fight. Feeders draw a card at Ebb when they share a reef with enemy warriors. Breeders add a zooid to your tail each Ebb: discard Kelp for a Swimmer, Coral for a Stinger, Pearl for a Feeder, Moon for another Breeder.
  • Severed. If a middle zooid dies and the chain breaks, the part cut off from the Float drifts loose and loses a zooid every Ebb until it reconnects.

How it scores

  • At Ebb, score your span, the number of reefs the chain touches: 3 reefs score 1 VP, 5 score 2, 7 score 3, 9 or more score 5.
  • Coil: 2 VP when your chain holds every neighbor of a single reef.

A turn

Flood
Loose pieces touching the chain rejoin it.
Slack
Slither and battle.
Ebb
Score span and coils, breed, loose pieces decay.

Field notePraya dubia can grow past 40 meters, longer than a blue whale, and each part of it is a separate animal doing one job.

HuntsSilver Shoal. FearsBlood Tide, Sandwright sandbars.

Plate IX · Programmer

The Octarch

Enteroctopus dofleini · giant Pacific octopus

Complexity
Reach
5

“Eight arms. Eight opinions.”

One giant octopus whose eight arms each think for themselves. You program every arm separately, and they carry out their orders in number order, whether or not those orders still make sense.

How it plays

  • Eight minds. At Flood, slot one or two cards under your arms, one card per arm. Every programmed arm acts each turn, arm 1 first. A Kelp arm reaches: it moves one channel, ignoring rule. A Coral arm grapples: it battles where it is. A Pearl arm pilfers: it takes a token, item or random card from its reef. A Moon arm picks any of the three.
  • Reach. Arms must stay within two channels of the Mantle, your head. An arm pushed out of reach snaps back to it.
  • Recoil. If an arm can't carry out its order, it recoils: discard its card and the cards of every higher-numbered arm, and lose 1 VP per card discarded.
  • Ink. Before dice are rolled against an arm or the Mantle, spend an ink to cancel the battle and slide that piece one channel away. Regain one ink each Ebb.
  • Regrowth. Lost arms grow back at the Mantle at your Ebb.

How it scores

  • Octopus's garden: at Ebb, 1 VP per different kind of treasure in your dens, such as each item type, shells, Lures, pigments, current arrows and sandbars.
  • Pilfering an enemy token also scores the usual 1 VP for removing it.
  • Dens score when built: 1, 2, 3.

A turn

Flood
Program arms. The Mantle may jet to any reef holding one of your arms.
Slack
Arms act, 1 to 8. Build a den where the Mantle is.
Ebb
Score the garden, regrow arms, draw 1 card plus 1 per den.

Field noteAbout two thirds of an octopus's neurons are in its arms. Each arm can taste, decide and grab on its own.

Huntsevery faction with tokens. FearsBlood Tide.

Plate X · Illusionist

The Chromatic Court

Sepia apama · giant cuttlefish

Complexity
Reach
4

“Trust your eyes. Everyone else does.”

Cuttlefish change color in a fraction of a second. The Court repaints the reef itself: a Kelp reef turns Coral for everyone, and every other faction's plans shift with it.

How it plays

  • Pigment. Place a Pigment in a reef with one of your cuttlefish. That reef's suit becomes the pigment's suit for everyone: building, crafting, card effects, spawning, blooming. Anyone ruling the reef can wash it off by discarding a card of the new suit.
  • Camouflage. Your cuttlefish in pigmented reefs can't be attacked unless the attacker discards a card of that reef's suit to spot them.
  • Hypnosis. Once per turn, in a reef with your Salon, walk up to two enemy warriors into an adjacent reef, even under a Lure or into a jellyfish smack.
  • Sneakers. In reefs with your camouflaged cuttlefish, your Undertow ambush cards count as any suit.

How it scores

  • Three face-up Gallery cards show patterns, such as three connected reefs of one suit or one gate of each suit. At Ebb, score any pattern on the map that has your cuttlefish in every reef of it (2 to 4 VP), then reveal a new one.
  • Salons score when built: 1, 2, 3.

A turn

Flood
Move one Pigment to a reef with your cuttlefish. Up to three can be in play.
Slack
3 actions: move, battle, recruit, pigment, build a Salon. Hypnosis once.
Ebb
Score galleries, draw a card.

Field noteSmall male giant cuttlefish sometimes disguise themselves as females to slip past bigger rivals.

Huntsanyone whose plan depends on suits. FearsOctarch.

Food web

Who hurts whom

Every faction is built to press on at least two others, so no lineup plays out the same way twice.

  • Sandwright Clans eat the Coral Commonwealth. Every coral head they graze turns into sand for their sandbars.
  • The Blood Tide follows the Silver Shoal. Fish are the easiest meal on the map, and every fight leaves Blood behind.
  • The Immortal Bloom punishes anything that must move. Sharks have to swim every turn and schools have to cross the map.
  • The Lantern Abyss baits everyone, and fish and sharks chase light first.
  • The Octarch collects everyone's tokens: Lures, pigments, shells, current arrows and sandbars all end up in its garden.
  • The Chromatic Court repaints suits, which breaks Coral spawning, Bloom timing and anyone's crafting plan.
  • The Hermit Syndicate arms the underdog. Whoever is losing buys shells, which drags the leader back.
  • The Siphon Chain walls off the middle, and a single shark bite cuts it in two. Sandbars stop both the Chain and jellyfish drift.

The app

On the phone, and how I'll build it

  • Solo against bots. Pick your faction and the rest are AI. Each faction gets its own bot that plays by its rules, weighing the points it can score against the pieces it could lose.
  • Pass-and-play. One phone, handed around. A cover screen hides your hand while the phone changes hands.
  • Save anywhere. The game saves after every action, so a 90-minute game can be finished over several sittings.
  • Landscape board with pinch to zoom. Your faction board and hand slide up from the bottom.
  • Rules at hand. Tap any piece, card or token to see the rule behind it.
  • Same install as Voice Music. Native Kotlin app, built on GitHub and downloaded to your phone from a release link.
  1. Engine and first pair. Map, cards, rule, movement, battle, crafting, Dominance, saving, pass-and-play. Blood Tide and Coral Commonwealth with bots. First playable APK.
  2. The movers. Silver Shoal, Immortal Bloom and Sandwright Clans, with bots.
  3. The schemers. Hermit Syndicate, Lantern Abyss and Siphon Chain, with bots.
  4. The hard two, then balance. Octarch and Chromatic Court. A simulator plays thousands of bot-vs-bot games to find factions that win too often, and I tune the numbers.
