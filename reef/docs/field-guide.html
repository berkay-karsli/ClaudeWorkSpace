Design draft 3 · softlock review · balance from bot games

Reef

An asymmetric strategy game for Android, built on Root's bones and set on a coral reef. 14 factions fight over twelve reefs, and each one is built around a single idea.

14 factions 2–4 per game 60–90 minutes Solo vs AI bots Pass-and-play on one phone 30 VP to win

Draft 3

Draft 3

Balance from bot games

Bots played 126 games across lineups of 2 to 4 factions. Every game finished, no player was ever left without a choice, and no piece or card was ever lost. These were the scores out of line, and the fixes, each written into its plate below.

After these changes every faction scores 2.0 to 2.8 VP a round against the bots, and a game usually ends around round 10 (5 to 29 in the bot games).

Since draft 1

What changed

Your four answers, then every inconsistency I found by checking each rule against all the others.

  • Four new factions: Sea Turtles, Starfish, Lionfish and Remoras join the original ten.
  • Simpler names: the game is Reef, each faction is named after its animal, the turn is Dawn, Day and Dusk, and every reef has a one- or two-word name.
  • Octopus and Cuttlefish keep their depth. They lose side rules that had nothing to do with their core idea.
  • First playable pair: Sharks against Coral. They're the simplest attacker and the simplest builder, and together they use moving, battles, building, crafting and cards, which is most of the engine.

Shared rules that were inconsistent

  • Pieces

    Draft 1: Draft 1 never defined a token. Blood had no owner, and sandbars and current arrows sat on channels where no rule could reach them.

    Now: Four kinds of piece, each with fixed rules. Blood is neutral, and channel markers can't be hit.

  • Crafting

    Draft 1: Every faction could craft items, but no plate said what it crafts with.

    Now: Only buildings craft, so only the three factions that build can craft.

  • Hits

    Draft 1: Stings, snaps, bait balls and grazing removed pieces with no shared definition, and Blood only counted battles.

    Now: An attack is anything that deals hits. Blood and remora scraps follow any attack.

  • Card draw

    Draft 1: Some plates repeated “draw a card at Dusk” and others didn't.

    Now: Everyone draws 1 card at Dusk. Plates list only extra draws.

  • Undefined words

    Draft 1: Jellyfish used “round” and anglerfish used “two channels from the Trench”, but neither was defined.

    Now: A round is defined, and the Trench touches exactly the three rim reefs.

  • Suit names

    Draft 1: Coral was both a card suit and a faction.

    Now: The suit is now Sponge.

  • Reach

    Draft 1: Reach claimed to measure how much map a faction holds, but the anglerfish hold none, and quiet lineups with no fighting were allowed.

    Now: Reach now measures pressure on other factions, with new minimums per lineup.

Factions that were inconsistent or doing too much

  • Sharks

    Draft 1: “No homes”, yet they built Nurseries. Cards “paid for actions”, yet they also had 3 free ones. One rule scored only against the Shoal.

    Now: No buildings: new sharks arrive from the open ocean. Cards buy one extra action, like every faction with a fixed number of actions.

  • Sardines

    Draft 1: Merged schools lost track of the gate they came in by. Their plate said they love the jellyfish's currents while the food web said jellyfish hunt them.

    Now: The game tracks every fish. Every relationship now comes from a rule.

  • Coral

    Draft 1: Three coral types, Calcium, bleaching, spawning, battles and two scoring tracks. Its stinging cells copied the jellyfish sting.

    Now: One coral type. It grows and spawns and never starts a fight.

  • Jellyfish

    Draft 1: “You never command them”, yet they could battle and build stalks. Returning jellyfish needed stalks that might not exist.

    Now: They bend currents and bloom, nothing else.

  • Parrotfish

    Draft 1: Sand came from ruling reefs, not from chewing. Cays blocked everyone without saying who rules them. A cocoon rule added a fifth system.

    Now: Sand comes only from grazing, and islands belong to the parrotfish.

  • Hermit Crabs

    Draft 1: Shells were armor for sale, the crabs' own armor and a conveyor line at once. “Each market moves the line” contradicted “the line moves when a large shell arrives.” They sold items they had no way to make.

    Now: One pool of shells that goes out and comes back.

  • Anglerfish

    Draft 1: Lure count was “your Glow”, but an upgrade also gave +1 lure, and Glow was spent as money too. Their Gullet clashed with jellyfish immortality. “Fish and sharks chase light” had no rule behind it.

    Now: No Glow. Extra lures unlock by eating.

  • Siphon Chain

    Draft 1: “Loose pieces touching the chain” and “walls off the middle” had no rules behind them, on top of four zooid types.

    Now: Now the Sea Snake: the same one-body idea with an animal everyone knows, in three rules.

  • Octopus

    Draft 1: Arms stole items from reefs, but items live on faction boards, and sandbars, which sit on channels. Dens, ink, regrowth, garden and recoil all ran at once.

    Now: Arms steal tokens and cards. Dens and ink are gone.

  • Cuttlefish

    Draft 1: Pigments were placed at both Dawn and Day, across six systems: paint, camouflage, hypnosis, sneakers, salons and galleries.

    Now: Paint, camouflage and galleries.

  • Food web

    Draft 1: It said every faction presses on at least two others, but coral pressed on none. Several claims had no rule behind them.

    Now: Every “hurts” is generated from a rule and appears as a matching “hurt by” on the other plate.

Rules every faction now follows

  • One idea. Every faction is built around a single core idea and has exactly three special rules.
  • Only what it needs. 9 of the 14 factions never start a battle, and 11 never build. The table below shows what each one does and never does.
  • Shared words. Every rule is written with the same words: warrior, building, token, channel marker, attack, hit, rule. A sting is a hit, so a turtle's shell ignores it without a special case.
  • No names. No rule mentions another faction. Every interaction comes from the shared words.
  • Both sides agree. Every “hurts” on one plate appears as “hurt by” on the other.
  • No dead ends. Every faction always has a way to put pieces back on the map, however badly things go. The softlock review above checks each one, and bot games test it.
  • Always a way to win. Each faction scores in one or two ways, both from its core idea, and can reach 30 VP in any lineup it's allowed in.

Shared rules

How the reef works

Root players will recognize most of this. The rules tagged reef are new, and several factions are built around them.

Twelve reefs

Each reef has a suit (Kelp, Sponge or Pearl) and one to three slots for buildings. Channels connect neighboring reefs.

Edges of the mapreef

The four corner reefs are gates to the open ocean. The top row is the shore. The three reefs touching the Trench are the rim.

A turn

Dawn, Day, Dusk. At Dusk every faction draws a card and discards down to five. A round is one turn for every faction.

Pieces

Warriors fight and count for rule. Buildings fill slots and count for rule. Tokens sit in a reef and don't count. Channel markers sit on channels, one per channel, and can't be hit. Removed pieces go back to their owner's supply.

Rule and moving

You rule a reef when you have the most warriors and buildings there; a tie means nobody does. A move takes any number of your warriors to a neighboring reef, and you must rule where you leave or where you arrive. Placing pieces from your supply isn't moving.

Currentsreef

Moving along a current never needs rule. Four currents circle the middle of the map as the Gyre.

Attacks and hits

An attack is a battle or any ability that deals hits. Each hit removes one of the target's pieces in that reef, warriors first, and its owner picks which. Removing an enemy building or token scores 1 VP. Removing warriors scores nothing unless a plate says so.

Battle

Pick a reef with your warriors and a faction with pieces there. Roll two dice showing 0 to 3: you deal the higher, the defender deals the lower, each capped by its warriors there. A defender with no warriors takes 1 extra hit. An Ambush card of the reef's suit deals 2 hits before the roll.

Cards

54 cards in Kelp, Sponge, Pearl and wild Moon. Once per turn, a faction with a fixed number of Day actions may discard a card for one more.

Gear

Each building can craft once per turn, paying with its reef's suit. Gear scores points and gives a small lasting bonus. Factions without buildings can't craft.

Winning

First to 30 VP. Once you have 10 VP you may play a Dominance card instead: you stop scoring, and you win if you rule three reefs of its suit at the start of your turn (Moon: two opposite gates).

Plates run from easiest to learn to hardest. A dot means the faction does it; a dash means it never does. Move: moves its warriors from reef to reef by choice. Battle: starts dice battles. Build: places buildings. Recruit: puts warriors from its supply onto the map, by any means. Craft: only buildings craft, so only builders craft.

Specimen plates

The factions in detail

Complexity runs from one to four dots. Reach is how much pressure a faction puts on the others. Each plate's Hurts and Hurt by lines come from the same list, so they always match.

Plate I · Predator

Sharks

Carcharhinus amblyrhynchos · grey reef sharks

Complexity
Reach
9

“Stop swimming and you drown.”

Sharks own nothing and build nothing. They roam, they fight, and they score from the blood that every fight leaves behind.

MoveBattleBuild (never)RecruitCraft (never)

Its three rules

  • Keep swimming. At Dusk, every shark that didn't move this turn drowns.
  • Big. Each shark counts as two warriors whenever warriors are counted, including for rule and for the hits you can deal.
  • Blood in the water. While sharks are in the game, any attack that removes a warrior leaves a Blood token in that reef, one per reef. Blood belongs to no one. Your sharks can move to a Blood reef up to two channels away, ignoring rule.

Pieces

10 sharks. 8 Blood tokens. Setup: 3 sharks at any gate.

A turn

Dawn
Sharks arrive at one gate: 1, plus 1 for each Blood token on the map, up to 3.
Day
3 actions: hunt. A hunt moves sharks from one reef, then they may battle where they arrive.
Dusk
Feed, then any shark that stayed still drowns.

Scores

  • At Dusk, remove every Blood token in a reef with your sharks: 2 VP each.

Field noteMany sharks breathe by swimming, pushing water over their gills. For them, stopping means suffocating.

HurtsSardines (they can't fight back, and every fight leaves Blood); Parrotfish (two hits per shark); Sea Snake (one hit mid-body cuts it); Octopus (two hits per shark against lone arms).

Hurt byJellyfish (forced moves swim into stings); Lionfish (venom hits attackers first).

“Ten thousand fish, one direction.”

A race across the map. Schools come in through one gate and score by leaving through another. Sardines never start a fight and never stay long.

A plague that eats reefs down to rubble. Starfish never start a fight. They crawl in, strip the reef bare, and hitting them only makes more.

Plate V · Builder

Coral

Acropora cervicornis · staghorn coral

Complexity
Reach
4

“We never move. We grow.”

A living reef. It can't take a step or start a fight. It spreads by spawning into neighboring reefs and scores by growing.

Move (never)Battle (never)BuildRecruitCraft

Its three rules

  • Rooted. Polyps never move, not even along currents.
  • Spawn. Once per turn, discard a card: each reef of its suit with your coral puts 1 polyp into every neighboring reef. With a Moon card, every reef with your coral spawns. If you have no coral on the map, the spawn instead puts 2 polyps into one reef of the card's suit.
  • Living reef. When you are battled, each of your coral in that reef counts as a warrior for the hits you can deal.

Pieces

20 polyps. 15 coral (buildings). Setup: 2 coral and 3 polyps in one reef that isn't a gate, and 1 polyp in each neighboring reef.

A turn

Dawn
Nothing.
Day
3 actions: grow (discard a card of the reef's suit to build a coral in an empty slot of a reef you rule) or spawn.
Dusk
Draw 1 extra card per 5 coral on the map.

Scores

  • Each coral scores when grown: 1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3 and so on.
  • Gear crafted by your coral.

Field noteMany corals spawn on the same one or two nights a year, timed by the moon. That is why a Moon card makes every reef spawn.

Hurtsnobody directly.

Plate VI · Drifter

Jellyfish

Aurelia aurita · moon jellyfish

Complexity
Reach
5

“We can't steer. We bend the current.”

You never move a jellyfish. They drift every turn, and all you control is the current that carries them. Whatever swims into them gets stung.

Move (never)Battle (never)Build (never)RecruitCraft (never)

Its three rules

Dusk

Plate VII · Terraformer

Parrotfish

Bolbometopon muricatum · bumphead parrotfish

Complexity
Reach
7

“Every beach was a reef once.”

Parrotfish chew the reef into sand, then use the sand to reshape the map. Sandbars cut channels, and islands rise out of the sea.

MoveBattleBuild (never)RecruitCraft (never)

Its three rules

  • Graze. In a reef you rule, remove one enemy building or token and gain 3 Sand. If there is nothing to eat, chew the bare reef for 2 Sand, once per reef each turn.
  • Sandbar. Spend 2 Sand to put a sandbar on a channel next to a reef you rule. For everyone but you, the two reefs stop being neighbors. A faction with warriors at both ends can spend a move to dig it out.
  • Island. Spend 5 Sand to raise an island in a reef you rule that touches 2 of your sandbars. You rule it for the rest of the game, your parrotfish there can't be attacked, and the island can't be removed.

Pieces

16 parrotfish. 8 sandbars (channel markers). 3 islands. Setup: 4 parrotfish at any gate.

A turn

Dawn
2 parrotfish arrive in a reef you rule, or at a gate if you rule none.
Day
3 actions: move, battle, graze, place a sandbar or raise an island.
Dusk
Score islands.

Scores

  • 1 VP per sandbar placed, 2 VP per island raised.
  • At Dusk, 1 VP per island.

Field noteMuch of the white sand on tropical beaches has passed through a parrotfish. Bumpheads settle their fights by ramming heads.

HurtsCoral (grazing eats coral); Starfish (grazing eats Rubble); Hermit Crabs (grazing eats markets); Sea Turtles (grazing eats nests and eggs); Cuttlefish (grazing eats pigments); Jellyfish (sandbars stop drift); Sea Snake (sandbars block its path).

Hurt bySharks (two hits per shark); Lionfish (gorging, and venom against their battles).

Plate IX · Serpent

Sea Snake

Laticauda colubrina · banded sea krait

Complexity
Reach
6

“The more it eats, the longer it gets.”

One snake with one long body stretched across the map. It grows only by eating, and a single hit in the middle can cut it in two.

MoveBattleBuild (never)RecruitCraft (never)

Its three rules

Dusk

Scores

Field noteBanded sea kraits hunt eels in reef crevices, then come ashore to digest and lay eggs.

“Why swim when you can ride?”

Remoras don't fight, build or hold ground. They stick to other factions' warriors, go where they go, and eat the scraps of every kill.

“Every shell has a price.”

The reef's only shell shop. Other factions buy shells to protect their warriors, and every card they pay becomes an action for the crabs.

MoveBattle (never)BuildRecruitCraft

Its three rules

  • Shell shop. At the start of another faction's Day, it may buy shells from your pool at your price, paying in cards. “Eight arms. Eight opinions.”

One giant octopus whose eight arms think for themselves. Each arm keeps a standing order and carries it out every turn, in number order, whether or not it still makes sense. Whatever the arms steal goes into the octopus's garden.

MoveBattleBuild (never)RecruitCraft (never)

Its three rules

  • Orders. At Dawn, put 1 or 2 cards under arms with no order. The suit is the order. Kelp: reach, move that arm one channel, ignoring rule. Sponge: grab, battle in its reef. Pearl: steal, take an enemy token from its reef, or a random card from a faction with pieces there, into your garden. Moon: pick each time.
  • Recoil. Arms act from 1 to 8. If an arm can't carry out its order, discard the orders on it and on every higher arm, and lose 1 VP per order discarded.
  • Reach. Arms stay within 2 channels of the Mantle, your body. After the Mantle moves, arms out of reach snap back to it. Lost arms keep their orders, skip their turn, and regrow at the Mantle at Dusk.

The app

On the phone, and how I'll build it

  • Solo against bots. Pick your faction and the rest are AI. Each faction gets its own bot that plays by its three rules.
  • Pass-and-play. One phone, handed around. A cover screen hides your hand while the phone changes hands.
  • Save anywhere. The game saves after every action, so a 90-minute game can be finished over several sittings.
  • Landscape board with pinch to zoom. Your faction board and hand slide up from the bottom.
  • Rules at hand. Tap any piece or card to see the rule behind it.
  • Installs like Voice Music. A native Kotlin app, built on GitHub and downloaded to your phone from a release link.
  1. Engine and first pair. Map, cards, rule, moving, attacks, crafting, Dominance, saving and pass-and-play. Sharks and Coral with bots. First playable APK.
  2. Moving and hitting. Sardines, Lionfish, Starfish and Jellyfish, with bots.
  3. Shaping and riding. Parrotfish, Sea Turtles, Sea Snake and Remoras, with bots.
  4. The schemers. Hermit Crabs, Anglerfish, Octopus and Cuttlefish, with bots.
  5. Balance. A simulator plays thousands of bot-vs-bot games to find factions that win too often, and I tune the numbers.
